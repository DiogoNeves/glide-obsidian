Purpose: keep Glide's internal memory separate from the owner's writing and input.

## Conversation is the default

`Agent HQ/` is agent-maintained. The owner may query or inspect its readable files but never needs to edit its views, queues, review packets or records. `Now`, `Ongoing` and `Durable` are retrieval views for the agent; do not assign opening or maintaining them as the owner's routine.

Bring a question, proposed change or review into the current conversation or configured notification channel. Include enough context, relevant evidence, the choice needed and its consequence to answer there. Internal links are optional supporting detail, never the only place containing required input. Queue lower-priority questions internally and surface them when useful; no reply leaves a decision pending. The agent records the answer and resulting state. If delivery fails, record that failure separately from content preparation, keep the question pending, and use only an already authorized fallback or the next available conversation. A saved internal question is not evidence of delivery; do not invent a new notification channel or retry indefinitely.

## When a shared document helps

Use a document only when requested or when sustained drafting/review makes one useful. Place it outside `Agent HQ/` among the owner's ordinary vault notes, at the vault root by default and following existing unique-note, naming, linking and template conventions. Use a different destination only when the owner's established convention or explicit choice calls for it; do not invent a collaboration folder. If no destination or edit scope is authorized, keep the review in conversation while resolving those details. Routine questions need no new note. Do not export every queue item, internal draft or memory record into the owner's files.

Mark a new collaborative document with small frontmatter such as `ai_assisted: true` and `ai_scope: Draft section`, plus a visible opening sentence explaining AI involvement and which sections the agent may edit. Keep AI drafts and interpretations distinguishable from owner-authored responses. Neither a label nor the document's location grants write authority. Edit only the authorized document/sections, retain user wording, and re-read for intervening owner edits before applying a bounded change. Do not add these properties to existing original writing without authorization. If an owner has edited an AI section, preserve those changes too; clarify an uncertain edit scope in conversation or offer a patch.

The memory broker does not provide a general writer for the owner's documents. Use an available, separately authorized narrow capability for a shared-file edit. If it is unavailable, keep the draft and input in conversation pending that scoped capability; do not relax whole-workspace protection or send the owner into HQ to work around it.

Keep the shared document a source or working surface, with a reference to its related managed record when useful. Durable memory, proposal IDs, evidence and revision receipts remain internal; do not create competing authoritative copies or rewrite the owner's text to match an internal interpretation.

An explicitly approved project-progress category retains its separate bounded permission to create new unique notes. It does not grant collaborative editing authority or permission to revise existing writing.

## Decisions and receipts

A document edit, checked box or local UI selection is evidence of input, not a runtime approval or completed action. Read it in context, resolve ambiguous intent conversationally, and follow the existing authorized proposal → revision validation → apply → receipt path. Retain AI authorship after approval. External actions still require their existing authorization; relocating a draft grants none.

## Review this boundary

Daily, weekly and harness reviews should check that required input reached the owner in conversation, no internal file became homework, and collaborative changes stayed within their recorded scope. Quiet internal maintenance does not require a new owner-facing note. Preserve useful history; correct active routing rather than moving old internal archives wholesale.
